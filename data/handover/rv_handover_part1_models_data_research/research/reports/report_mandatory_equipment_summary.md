# Mandatory equipment for an Indian camper (research report)

> Copied from the project chat as delivered (text chat, 20–21 Nov 2026 IST). Reproduced from the chat text visible to the packager; wording kept as delivered. Links kept as delivered.

Here's what an Indian camper must have fitted or carried. In short, it has to meet the motor caravan standard (AIS-124) and carry the kit every vehicle carries. State tourism policies only add extras if you register it for tourism.

**Mandatory for every camper**
- **What makes it a camper:** fixed seats and a table, a sleeping area, cooking facilities and storage. The definition is in Rule 2(za), and Rule 125G with AIS-124 makes the standard binding.
- **Fire extinguishers:** two of 2 kg each, one near the driver, mounted securely in a marked spot. That's AIS-124 clause 3.4.
- **First aid kit:** antiseptic cream, sterile dressings, plasters, gauze and a bandage. That's Rule 138(4)(d).
- **Warning triangles:** red reflective ones that meet AIS-022. That's Rule 138(4)(c).
- **Spare tyre:** required by Rule 138(4)(a). A smaller car-category (M1) camper can skip it if it has run-flat tyres, or tubeless tyres plus a repair kit.
- **Tool kit:** the one the manufacturer specifies, under Rule 138(4)(b).
- **Electrical wiring:** it must be properly secured and meet the national wiring standard, IS 2465 (AIS-124 clause 3.5). If the living area has its own electrics, you also need fused appliances, 24 hours of battery or generator backup, and a 230-volt hook-up point (clause 4.5.3).
- **Beds and seats:** beds must be fixed in place, and nobody may ride in the living area except in seats with belts. That's AIS-124 clauses 4.2 and 3.5.3.
- **FASTag:** required by Rule 138A.

**Depends on the vehicle**
- **Wheel chocks:** one if the camper's loaded weight is over 4 tonnes, two if it's over 7.5 tonnes (Rule 138(4)(e)).
- **Emergency exit:** required on bus-size (M3) campers under Rule 128A.
- **Rear underrun guard:** this is the bar under the back that stops a car sliding underneath. It applies to the bigger M2 and M3 campers.
- **Speed limiter, and a GPS tracker with a panic button:** only needed if the camper is registered for commercial use (Rules 118 and 125H).

**Not required by the central rules**
- **Mud flaps:** not required, because the rule that asks for them leaves campers out.
- **Cooking gas:** there's no standard for the gas cylinder or how it's fitted. The only requirement is that the cooker is fixed in place.
- **Bed size and ventilation:** no rules set these.

**Registration and fitness**
- **Converting an existing vehicle:**
  - The base vehicle can be at most 3 years old.
  - The chassis, engine, brakes, suspension and fuel system must stay unchanged.
  - Only the manufacturer, its dealer or a state-approved workshop may do the work.
  - The workshop issues a Form 22F certificate, and you file Form 22D with the RTO within 14 days (Rules 112 and 47B).
- **New camper:** it's registered under Rule 47 with Form 22B.
- **Fitness certificate:** only needed for commercial registration (Rule 62). It's renewed every 2 years until the vehicle is 8 years old, then every year. A private camper doesn't need one.

**State tourism policies**
- **Kerala (2021), Goa (2023) and Maharashtra (2021):** they add extras such as a toilet, AC, GPS and a fridge, but only for campers registered with their tourism schemes.
- **Karnataka:** its policy only offers subsidies and tax breaks.
- **Himachal Pradesh:** I found no caravan policy.

**Packager's note (21 Nov 2026, added, not part of the original report):** GSR 596(E) of 26 Aug 2021 has since been found and downloaded (gazette copy, see /rules/official_pdfs/).
