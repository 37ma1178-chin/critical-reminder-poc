# Recreational vehicle, motor caravan and camper van rules in India: research for the ObjectWeave RV India regulatory layer

- **Prepared:** 8 Oct 2026 (IST)
- **Scope:** Central law (Motor Vehicles Act 1988, Central Motor Vehicles Rules 1989 (CMVR), MoRTH S.O./G.S.R. notifications), technical standards (AIS), Supreme Court case law, and State/UT overlays (Kerala, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh).

## 1. Plain-language summary

1. **India does have a formal RV category.** CMVR **Rule 2(za)** defines a **"Motor Caravan"** as a *special purpose category M vehicle* with living accommodation that has at least seats and a table, sleeping space (which can convert from seats), cooking facilities and storage. All of these must be rigidly fixed; only the table may be removable. The definition was inserted by **G.S.R. 868(E), 8 Sep 2016**. Type approval is governed by **AIS-124:2014** through **CMVR Rule 125G**.
2. **Registration types already exist for camper vans and trailers.** Under **S.O. 1248(E), 5 Nov 2004** (issued under MV Act s.41(4)), "Camper van or trailer for private use" is a **non-transport** (private) vehicle type, and "Camper vans or trailers" (commercial) are **transport** vehicles. That notification defines a camper van as a "special purpose M1 category vehicle" with the same four minimum facilities.
3. **Factory-built and converted motor caravans are both allowed, with conditions.**
   - Motor caravans manufactured on or after **1 Apr 2018** must comply with AIS-124 (Rule 125G(1)).
   - **Conversions** of existing vehicles are allowed under AIS-124 clause 0.0(b) and Rule 125G(2)/(3). They must be done by a **motor caravan body builder** on a **registered vehicle not older than 3 years**, without altering the type-approved chassis, suspension, brakes, fuel system or engine. They need a **type approval certificate from a Rule 126 test agency** (ARAI, ICAT, VRDE, CIRT, GARC, NATRAX, etc.), and the change must be **endorsed on the RC under MV Act s.52 within 14 days**.
   - From **six months after 27 Aug 2021** (approximately late Feb 2022; exact date not confirmed), all "motor vehicles modified as Motor Caravans" must comply with AIS-124 (Rule 125G(3) and Rule 112(8), inserted by **G.S.R. 596(E), 26 Aug 2021**).
4. **Donor vehicles:**
   - **Vans (M1/N1), buses (M2/M3) and truck chassis (N category) can all be base vehicles.** AIS-124 explicitly allows a base vehicle of category M or N, complete or incomplete. The completed vehicle is always **category M (M1, M2 or M3)**.
   - **Towed/trailer caravans** are **not** covered by AIS-124. India currently has **no notified type-approval standard for caravan trailers towed by cars**: AIS-113 / Rule 125D only covers trailers towed by N2/N3 trucks. A draft trailer-caravan AIS was reported in **April 2026** but has not been confirmed as notified.
   - **Rule 112** (substituted 2021, w.e.f. 1 Apr 2021) requires every s.52 alteration (body structure, chassis, fuel, etc.) to be done by an **OEM, OEM dealer, or State-authorised workshop or service station**, with compliance tested by a Rule 126 agency or self-certified in **Form 22F**.
5. **What is NOT allowed:**
   - altering a vehicle so its RC particulars differ from the manufacturer's original specification without a prescribed route (MV Act s.52(1); **SC, *RTO v. K. Jayachandra*, 9 Jan 2019**);
   - changing chassis, suspension, brakes, engine or fuel system while building a motor caravan (AIS-124 cl. 0.1);
   - converting vehicles **older than 3 years** under AIS-124 (as published in 2014; whether Amendment 1 changed this was not checked);
   - exceeding Rule 93 dimensions (for M category: width 2.6 m, height 4.0 m, length 12 m for M1/M2, 13.5 m for a 2-axle M3, 15 m for a 3-axle M3) or the **60% of wheelbase overhang** limit (Rule 93(2));
   - letting anyone occupy the living area while moving, except in designated seats (AIS-124 cl. 3.5.3);
   - chassis swaps from a different manufacturer or type (Rule 112 proviso).
6. **Commercial use:**
   - Renting out or carrying tourists makes it a transport vehicle. That needs a permit (contract carriage / All India Tourist Permit under the **AITP Rules 2023, G.S.R. 302(E), 18 Apr 2023**), fitness certification and transport-vehicle tax.
   - The AITP is unavailable once the vehicle is **12 years** past first registration.
   - The **Rent-a-Cab Scheme 1989** only covers *motor cabs*, so there is **no clear central route for self-drive caravan rental**.
7. **States:**
   - States add tourism registration and incentives, and set **motor vehicle tax**.
   - **Kerala** taxes "Caravan/Camping Trailer" at **₹1,000 per m² of floor area per quarter**, halved for Tourism Department rental/contract caravans by the Kerala Finance Act 2022.
   - **Maharashtra** (GR 15 Mar 2021) requires Tourism Directorate registration for commercial caravans and promised a vehicle-tax exemption order (not located).
   - **Goa** requires RTO registration in Goa, AIS-124/AIS-113 compliance and a chassis no older than 3 years, and reimburses part of MV tax.
   - **Karnataka** lists an MV-tax exemption for caravans in its tourism policy material (details unverified).
   - **Madhya Pradesh** policy text was not found.

## 3. Q1: Is there a formal RV / motor caravan / camper van category?

**Yes, at central level, in three overlapping instruments.**

### 3.1 CMVR Rule 2(za): "Motor Caravan" (status: VERIFIED in substance; Gazette copy not retrieved)
- **Text:** "'Motor Caravan' means a special purpose category M vehicle constructed to include living accommodation which contains at least the following equipment: (i) seats and table; (ii) sleeping accommodation which may be converted from the seats; (iii) cooking facilities; and (iv) storage facilities, which shall be rigidly fixed to the living compartment: Provided that the table may be designed to be easily removable."
- **Inserted by:** **G.S.R. 868(E), dated 8 Sep 2016**, w.e.f. 8 Sep 2016. The same notification inserted R.2(zb) Road Ambulance, R.2(zc) School Bus and R.2(zd) SPV, inserted R.125F and R.125G, and amended Form 22A.
- **Related definition:** **Rule 2(zd) "Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)"** was substituted by **G.S.R. 596(E), 26 Aug 2021** (w.e.f. 27 Aug 2021). It now reads: "a motor vehicle so constructed or altered to serve as a specific transportation requirement of goods or passenger transport". The 2016 version referred to "category L…, M, N or T having specific technical features".
- **Vehicle categories, Rule 2(k)–(rf):**
  - M1 = up to 8 seats plus the driver.
  - M2 = 9 or more seats plus the driver, GVW up to 5 t.
  - M3 = 9 or more seats plus the driver, GVW over 5 t.
  - N1 = GVW up to 3.5 t; N2 = 3.5–12 t; N3 = over 12 t.
  - T = towed vehicle; T1 up to 0.75 t; T2 0.75–3.5 t; T3 3.5–10 t; T4 over 10 t.
- **Sources (evidence class):**
  - Official statutory text via annotated reprint: https://ebook.commerciallawpublishers.com/fa/cmvr/files/basic-html/page37.html (R.2(za)–(zd)); page 32–33 (categories).
  - India Code CMVR PDF (official; would not load from this environment): https://www.indiacode.nic.in/ViewFileUploaded?file=cmvr-1989.pdf&path=AC_BR_59_851_00005_00005_1712231779316%2Frulesindividualfile%2F
  - Gazette text of G.S.R. 868(E) is mirrored at https://indiacode.ecourtsindia.com/rules/a0428387/ (Secondary mirror; bot-check blocked full read; search snippet confirms wording).

### 3.2 S.O. 1248(E), 5 Nov 2004: types of vehicle for registration under MV Act s.41(4) (status: VERIFIED in substance; check for later supersession)
- **Transport vehicles** list item (xvi): "Camper vans or trailers".
- **Non-transport vehicles** list item (xi): "Camper van or trailer for private use".
- **Definition (c):** "'Camper van' means a special purpose M1 category vehicle constructed to include living accommodation which contains at least: seats and table; sleeping accommodation which may be converted from the seats; cooking facilities; and storage facilities. This equipment shall be rigidly fixed to the living compartment; however, the table may be designed to be easily removable."
- Amended by S.O. 558(E), 9 Feb 2018, which added non-transport item (xv) for SPVs used for health, education or environment promotion.
- **Discrepancy to note:** this notification says camper van = **M1**, while CMVR R.2(za) / AIS-124 say motor caravan = **category M** (any of M1/M2/M3). Create a conflict record rather than choosing one.
- **Source:** https://ebook.commerciallawpublishers.com/fa/cmvr/files/basic-html/page925.html to page927 (Official statutory text via reprint). A copy is also hosted at https://programacalac.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/S.O.1248Edated-5-11-2004.pdf (Secondary host).

### 3.3 S.O. 436(E), 12 Jun 1989: types of motor vehicle in the RC (status: UNKNOWN whether still operative)
- Lists "Campers van" and "Camping trailer/House trailer" under *Medium and heavy motor vehicle – Passenger vehicle*. Definitions:
  - (d) "Campers' van": a motor vehicle designed or constructed to provide living quarters for recreational camping or travel use, with direct walk-through access to the living quarters from the driver's seat.
  - (e) "Camping trailer": a trailer, not used for goods, with partial side walls that fold for towing and unfold to provide temporary living accommodation.
  - (g) "House trailer": a trailer or semi-trailer equipped and used for temporary living quarters for camping or tourist purposes, not for freight.
- The reprint still prints it, but S.O. 1248(E) is the later instrument. The relationship between them is unclear (S.O. 1248(E) expressly supersedes S.O. 451(E) of 1992, not S.O. 436(E)).
- **Source:** https://ebook.commerciallawpublishers.com/fa/cmvr/files/basic-html/page777.html to page780 (Official statutory via reprint).

### 3.4 AIS-124:2014: technical definition (status: VERIFIED, base text read)
- **Title:** "Procedure for Type Approval and Certification of Motor Caravans for compliance to Central Motor Vehicles Rules". ARAI for AISC under CMVR-TSC, MoRTH, July 2014.
- **Definitions:**
  - cl. 2.2: Motor caravan = SPV of category M with the same four minimum facilities.
  - cl. 2.3: **Trailer caravan** = category T vehicle providing living accommodation.
  - cl. 2.9: Motor caravan body builder.
- The introduction names "Recreational Vehicle (RV), Camper Vans, Motor Homes" as falling under "Caravan".
- **Amendment No. 1, 12 Jan 2021:** reportedly limits the scope's seating capacity to **12 excluding driver** (previously 13) and updates fire-extinguisher references. The Amd 1 text was **not read**. Sources: SelectRegs catalogue (Secondary) https://www.selectregs.com/catalogue/index/IND-1242014/IND-N-01 and ACMA reproduction of the CMVR-TSC agenda (Secondary) https://www.acma.in/uploads/otherdocmanager/60th_CMVR_TSC_Annexure.pdf

## 4. Q2: Which vehicle types can be converted, and on what conditions

### 4.1 Applicability (AIS-124 cl. 0.0)
"This standard is applicable to: a) Motor caravan manufactured by vehicle manufacturer. b) Motor caravan which are built by motor caravan body builder on registered vehicle which is not older than 3 years. This standard does not cover type approval requirements for trailer caravans."

### 4.2 Conversion constraints (AIS-124 cl. 0.1)
- "Any alteration or modification in already type approved vehicle to build motor caravan shall be carried out in accordance with sound engineering practices and in compliance with [MV Act 1988 and CMVR 1989]."
- "**Alterations or modifications shall not be done to the already type approved vehicle, vehicle systems and components e.g. chassis, suspensions, brakes, fuel system, engine etc. while building motor caravan.**"
- **Permitted exceptions** (Table 2 notes):
  - F: re-routing or lengthening the refuelling duct and repositioning the approved tank inboard;
  - H/Q: extending the exhaust after the last silencer by up to 2 m without further test.

### 4.3 Base vehicle category (AIS-124 Table 2 Note G)
- Requirements follow "the corresponding category of the base vehicle, based on the maximum mass (complete/incomplete vehicle of **either M or N** according to the respective category of base vehicle)".
- Test columns cover M1 ≤ 2,500 kg, M1 > 2,500 kg, M2 and M3.
- **Practical reading:** van, bus and truck chassis bases are all contemplated. The output is an M-category SPV. Status: CONDITIONAL; PROFESSIONAL VERIFICATION REQUIRED for N to M re-categorisation at the RTO.

### 4.4 Technical requirements beyond base CMVR (AIS-124 cl. 3–4)
- Two fire extinguishers of at least 2 kg each (IS 13849 / IS 2171), one near the driver. No halogenated agents.
- Wiring to IS 2465, looms and clips required.
- Seats, table, beds, kitchen and storage must be fixed permanently (bolted, riveted, screwed or welded).
- Lap belts at least for all rear designated seats. Non-travel seats must be marked.
- **Water:** separate grey tank; separate black tank if a fixed toilet is fitted.
- **Power:** generator or battery backup of at least 24 h; 230 V external input; fused appliances.
- Cooking may be inside or outside.
- The full CMVR compliance list (Table 1) covers: R.93 dimensions, R.95 tyres, R.96 brakes, R.98 steering, R.100 glass, R.124 components (incl. AIS-031 superstructure strength, IS 15061 flammability, AIS-047 interior fittings), R.125 belts and anchorages, and R.124(1-A) rear underrun protection, among others.

### 4.5 Procedure
- **Technical file:** the body builder submits technical changes to the test agency per AIS-007 tables (cl. 5.1.1), plus the base vehicle's TA certificate number and date (cl. 5.3–5.4).
- **Identification:** a motor caravan identification number must be punched (Annex C).
- **Registration (cl. 8):**
  - **Incomplete vehicle** (drive-away chassis): register via **CMVR Rule 47(1)(g)**, with Form 22 from the chassis maker and **Form 22A Part II** from the body builder. Since G.S.R. 868(E), Part II item 5 asks for "Vehicle Body construction (Bus/Road Ambulance/Motor Caravan/etc.) Type Approval Certificate Number … issued by the approved Test Agency".
  - **Already registered vehicle:** the owner applies to the registering authority **within 14 days** of alteration under **MV Act s.52** for endorsement (place and date of alteration, alteration certificate number). The body builder must also ensure this.
- **Approved test agencies (CMVR Rule 126):** VRDE Ahmednagar, ARAI Pune, CFMTTI Budni, IIP Dehradun, CIRT Pune, ICAT Manesar, NRFMTTI Hissar (combines only), GARC Chennai, NATRAX Indore, and others specified by the Centre. Procedure follows AIS-017.

### 4.6 CMVR Rule 125G: Type Approval of Special Purpose Vehicles
Status: VERIFIED in substance (text via annotated reprint). Source: https://ebook.commerciallawpublishers.com/fa/cmvr/files/basic-html/page332.html
- (1) Motor caravans manufactured on or after **1 Apr 2018** must comply with AIS-124:2014 as amended. Inserted by **G.S.R. 868(E), 8 Sep 2016**.
- (2) "The Motor Caravans built on the existing vehicles as permitted under the clause 0.0(b) of the said standard shall also comply with AIS-124:2014." Same notification.
- (3) "On and after six months from the date of commencement of Central Motor Vehicles (Eighteenth Amendment) Rules, 2021, **motor vehicles modified as Motor Caravans** shall comply with AIS 124:2014…". Inserted by **G.S.R. 596(E), 26 Aug 2021**, w.e.f. 27 Aug 2021.

### 4.7 CMVR Rule 112: Alterations to motor vehicle
Status: CONDITIONAL. Text via annotated reprint; the reprint footnote attributes the substitution to **G.S.R. 240(E), 31 Mar 2021, w.e.f. 1 Apr 2021**. **Verify against the Gazette.** Source: https://ebook.commerciallawpublishers.com/fa/cmvr/files/basic-html/page175.html and page176.html
- (1) Any alteration or retrofitment under s.52(1), "including but not limited to change in fuel, conversion of propulsion system, replacement of engine, replacement of chassis or **modification in body structure**", must comply with conditions and standards notified by the Centre. It must be carried out by (i) the OEM, (ii) an OEM dealer, (iii) a **State-authorised workshop** or (iv) a **State-authorised service station**.
- **Proviso:** chassis replacement is allowed only if the new chassis is from the same manufacturer and of the same type. "The conversion of chassis meant for one type of motor vehicle shall not be permitted for another type of motor vehicle."
  - **Ambiguity:** it is unclear whether this restricts only chassis *replacement* or also goods-to-passenger re-purposing. PROFESSIONAL VERIFICATION REQUIRED.
- (3) Compliance is tested and validated by Rule 126 agencies, or self-certified by the entity doing the work, with a **Form 22F** compliance certificate issued on the portal or on paper.
- (8) "On and after six months from the date of commencement of [CMV (Eighteenth Amendment) Rules, 2021], Motor Caravans shall comply with the requirements stated in AIS-124:2014". Inserted by G.S.R. 596(E). The reprint places this as sub-rule (8) of Rule 112.

### 4.8 MV Act 1988 s.52: alteration (as amended by Act 32 of 2019, w.e.f. 1 Sep 2019)
Status: VERIFIED in substance (Secondary bare-act copy). Sources: https://courtbook.in/search/section/section-52-alteration-in-motor-vehicle-66829fa328263b967f090825 (Secondary); official: India Code MV Act (not loaded here).
- **s.52(1):** "No owner of a motor vehicle shall so alter the vehicle that the particulars contained in the certificate of registration are at variance with those originally specified by the manufacturer".
  - Proviso 1: fuel or energy conversion kits, subject to prescribed conditions.
  - **Proviso 2 (substituted 2019):** "the Central Government may prescribe specifications, conditions for approval, retrofitment and other related matters for the alteration of motor vehicles and in such cases, the warranty granted by the manufacturer shall not be considered as void".
  - Proviso 3: the Centre "may grant exemption for alteration of vehicles in a manner other than specified above, for any specific purpose".
- **s.52(1A)** (2019): the Centre may direct manufacturers to retrofit safety equipment.
- **s.52(2)** (2019): conversion to an *adapted vehicle* (for persons with disabilities).
- **s.52(3):** an unapproved alteration must be reported within 14 days, with the RC, for entry.
- **Explanation:** "alteration" means "a change in the structure of a vehicle which results in a change in its basic feature".

### 4.9 Bus body code / accreditation
- **CMVR R.125C(1):** body building of buses with **13 or more passengers excluding driver** must follow **AIS-052 (Rev.1)**, in phases from 2017–18.
- The Central Motor Vehicles (Accreditation of Bus Body Builders) Order, 2012 governs bus body builders.
- A motor caravan carries the AIS-124 TA certificate instead. AIS-124 Amd 1's reported 12-seat cap keeps motor caravans below the bus-code threshold (Secondary on Amd 1).
- **Correction to the brief:** **AIS-093 is the truck cabin/body code** (CMVR R.125C(3): N2/N3 goods vehicles, AIS-093 (Rev.1)-2015). It is *not* the bus body code; **AIS-052 is**.
- Source: https://ebook.commerciallawpublishers.com/fa/cmvr/files/basic-html/page324.html to page326.html (Official statutory via reprint).

### 4.10 Rule numbers named in the brief that do not apply as assumed
- **Rule 126E:** no such rule exists in the CMVR compilation (amended to Apr 2023). Rules 126B–126D are "[Omitted]".
- **Rules 95/96** are **tyres** and **brakes**, not GVW or axle limits. Axle and GVW limits are set under **MV Act s.58** by **S.O. 3467(E), 16 Jul 2018**.

## 5. Q3: Dimension, weight and axle limits

### 5.1 CMVR Rule 93: overall dimensions
Substituted by **G.S.R. 414(E), 26 Jun 2020**, replacing old Rules 93, 93A, 93B and 93C; later provisos by G.S.R. 435(E) of 18 Jun 2021, G.S.R. 161(E) of 25 Feb 2022 and G.S.R. 200(E) of 14 Mar 2022. Status: VERIFIED in substance (text via reprint). Source: https://ebook.commerciallawpublishers.com/fa/cmvr/files/basic-html/page137.html to page144.html

| Category | Max width (m) | Max length (m) | Max height (m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | 2.6 | 12.0 | 4.0 |
| M2 | 2.6 | 12.0 | 4.0 |
| M3, 2 axles (incl. double deck) | 2.6 | 13.5 | 4.0 (double-deck 4.75) |
| M3, 3 axles | 2.6 | 15.0 | 4.0 |
| Sleeper coach, 2 / 3 axles | 2.6 | 13.5 / 15.0 | 4.0 |
| N1 (2 axles) | 2.6 | 6.5 | 3.0 |
| N2 | 2.6 | 12.0 | 4.0 |
| N3 | 2.6 | 12.0 | 4.0 |
| Semi-trailer with tractor; truck-trailer / tractor-trailer | 2.6 | 18.75 | 4.0 |

- **Exclusions from measurement:** for width, mirrors, rub-rails up to 20 mm and side foot-steps. For length, ladders, lamps, number plates, spare wheels, bumpers, towing hooks and similar.
- **For M category, overall height excludes the air-conditioning unit** (Explanation III). This matters for roof AC on motorhomes.
- **Overhang, Rule 93(2):** "The overhang of the motor vehicle other than a tractor, construction equipment vehicle and combine harvester shall not exceed **60 percent of the wheel base**."
- **Note on Rule 93:** "special purpose vehicles exempted by general or special order of registering authority shall be exempt from the height requirements". This is relevant only if a registering authority issues such an order.
- **Rule 128** (tourist vehicle specifications) expressly excludes "campers, van, house trailer" from its tourist-coach body specifications.
- AIS-124 Table 2 makes "Overall dimensions" a test item (T) for converted caravans, so any change in dimensions is re-verified.

### 5.2 Weights and axles
- **MV Act s.58 and S.O. 3467(E), 16 Jul 2018** (supersedes S.O. 728(E), 1996). Maximum safe axle weight for **transport vehicles (other than motor cabs)**:
  - single axle, single tyres: 3.0 t; single axle, two tyres: 7.5 t; single axle, four tyres: 11.5 t;
  - tandem (axles less than 1.8 m apart): 21 t; tri-axle (outer axles less than 3 m apart): 27 t;
  - +1 t per axle with pneumatic suspension;
  - GVW capped at 49 t (rigid) and 55 t (semi-articulated / truck-trailer);
  - 5% GVW tolerance for s.113(3).
  - Source: https://ebook.commerciallawpublishers.com/fa/cmvr/files/basic-html/page929.html to page930.html (Official statutory via reprint).
- **Private (non-transport) motorhomes:** S.O. 3467(E) is framed for transport vehicles. For private caravans the binding limit is the manufacturer's GVW and axle ratings on the base vehicle's TA and RC, which AIS-124 forbids altering (cl. 0.1). AIS-124 Table 1 item 1: axle loading "shall comply provisions laid down in CMVR". Status: CONDITIONAL.
- **Category thresholds that matter for design:**
  - M2/M3 boundary is 5 t GVW (R.2(m),(n)).
  - LMV = GVW up to **7,500 kg** (MV Act s.2(21) as amended 2019). The Supreme Court Constitution Bench in *Bajaj Allianz v. Rambha Devi*, **2024 INSC 840 (6 Nov 2024)**, held that an LMV licence holder may drive a transport vehicle with GVW up to 7,500 kg. Source: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2017/29686/29686_2017_1_1501_57018_Judgement_06-Nov-2024.pdf (Official judicial; holding confirmed via search summary, full text not read).
  - Driving licence class mapping: **S.O. 2159(E), 28 May 2021**: M1/N1 = LMV; M2/M3 = medium/heavy passenger. Source: page931–933 of the reprint.
- **Speed limits, S.O. 1522(E), 6 Apr 2018:** M1: 120 km/h on access-controlled expressways, 100 on 4-lane divided roads, 70 elsewhere. M2/M3: 100 / 90 / 60. Source: page928 (via reprint).

### 5.3 Trailers
- **MV Act s.2(28):** "motor vehicle" includes a trailer. **s.2(46):** trailer = a vehicle (other than a semi-trailer or side-car) drawn by a motor vehicle. So trailers must be registered. *(Statute text not re-read in this pass; standard definitions; mark VERIFY.)*
- **CMVR R.47(1)(c):** registration of a **trailer other than a category T vehicle** must include the State Transport Authority / Transport Commissioner's design approval proceedings.
- **CMVR R.125D:** from 1 Apr 2016, type approval of **category T used for goods** towed by **N2/N3** follows **AIS-113:2013**; couplings follow IS 8007 / AIS-091 and AIS-092. AIS-113 does not cover M-category tow vehicles.
- **CMVR R.126** requires prototype testing for manufacturers or importers of trailers and semi-trailers.
- **Light trailers:** no CMVR provision found that sets a maximum towed mass for cars. S.O. 2159(E) (DL classes) maps Indian classes to Geneva 1949 / Vienna 1968 classes, where Class B may tow a "light trailer" (Vienna: up to 750 kg, or more under combined-mass conditions). This is an equivalence table for international driving permits, not a domestic towing rule. Status: UNKNOWN for a domestic car-towing limit; use the vehicle manufacturer's rated towing capacity as the engineering limit (Manufacturer evidence).
- **Draft trailer-caravan AIS:** reported 26–27 Apr 2026. Covers RVs, camper vans, motor homes and trailer caravans: sleeping, seating and table, cooking and storage. AIS number not published in the sources found. Sources: Economic Times, 27 Apr 2026 12:21 AM IST, https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/services/travel/boost-for-tourism-draft-rules-finalised-for-trailer-caravans/articleshow/130538259.cms (Secondary); currato / TOI syndication, 26 Apr 2026, https://currato.com/govt-proposes-standards-for-tractor-acs-troop-carrier-trailer-caravan-approval-india-news/ (Secondary). **Status: UNKNOWN whether finalised or notified as of 8 Oct 2026.**

## 7. Q5: Donor vehicle legality

### 7.1 *Regional Transport Officer & Ors. v. K. Jayachandra & Anr.*, Supreme Court of India
- **Citation:** Civil Appeal Nos. 219–222 of 2019 with C.A. 223/2019; judgment of **9 Jan 2019** by Arun Mishra and Vineet Saran, JJ.; reported as [2019] 1 SCR 179.
- **Status:** Official judicial; VERIFIED (read).
- **Source:** https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2013/6343/6343_2013_Judgement_09-Jan-2019.pdf
- **Facts:** Kerala owners built bodies that extended vehicle length beyond the chassis or manufacturer's specification, and RTOs refused registration. The Kerala HC Division Bench sided with the owners based on the Rules. The SC reversed it.
- **Holdings:**
  - "No vehicle can be altered in a manner where particulars in the certificate of registration are at variance with those 'originally specified by the manufacturer'" (para 27).
  - "Alterations which do not change the basic features are outside the purview of alteration" (para 27).
  - The Rules (incl. CMVR R.93 and R.126, and Kerala rr. 96, 103, 261) "cannot be so interpreted so as to permit the alteration as prohibited under section 52(1)" (para 32).
  - The Rules are subservient to the Act (para 33).
- **Effect after 2019 and 2021:** the judgment interprets s.52 as it stood before Act 32 of 2019. The 2019 substituted second proviso lets the Centre prescribe conditions for "the alteration of motor vehicles". The Centre then made **Rule 112 (2021)** and **Rule 112(8) / 125G(3)**, which specifically contemplate "motor vehicles modified as Motor Caravans". *Inference (not judicially tested; PROFESSIONAL VERIFICATION REQUIRED):* converting a vehicle into a motor caravan through an authorised workshop, with AIS-124 TA and RC endorsement, is now a prescribed route under s.52. Changes outside that route (chassis extension, wheelbase or overhang changes, suspension or brake changes) remain prohibited by s.52(1) as read in *Jayachandra*, and by AIS-124 cl. 0.1.

### 7.2 Goods vehicle or bus to camper
- **Bus (M2/M3) to motor caravan:** contemplated by AIS-124 (M2/M3 columns), subject to the 3-year age limit and no changes to the running gear. Status: CONDITIONAL.
- **Goods vehicle (N) to motor caravan:** AIS-124 Note G allows N-category base vehicles. The completed vehicle becomes M-category. Status: CONDITIONAL; PROFESSIONAL VERIFICATION REQUIRED. Open issues:
  - (a) the Rule 112 proviso on converting a chassis "meant for one type of motor vehicle" to another;
  - (b) RTO practice on changing class from goods carrier to camper van;
  - (c) GVW and axle and seat-belt anchorage implications.
- **Change of vehicle class / transport vs non-transport:** S.O. 1248(E) provides both "Camper van or trailer for private use" (non-transport) and "Camper vans or trailers" (transport). Moving from a transport class (e.g., goods carrier) to a non-transport camper changes tax treatment; Kerala's schedule has a reclassification provision in its one-time tax annexure, for example. Process: Form 20/RC endorsement per State practice. Status: UNKNOWN in detail.
- **Older donors (over 3 years):** no AIS-124 route as published. Status: UNSUPPORTED (pending Amd 1 text check).

### 7.3 Body builder requirements
- AIS-124 needs a "motor caravan body builder" (a firm making caravan bodies) whose design holds a TA certificate from a Rule 126 agency.
- Rule 112(1) requires the alteration to be done by an OEM, OEM dealer, or State-authorised workshop or service station.
- There is **no separate central accreditation scheme for caravan builders** comparable to the Bus Body Builder Accreditation Order 2012. None was found. Status: UNKNOWN.

### 7.4 Fuel and chassis rules
- **Chassis, suspension, brakes, engine, fuel system:** no changes when building a motor caravan (AIS-124 cl. 0.1). Allowed exceptions: tank repositioned inboard and filler re-routed (Note F); exhaust tail extension up to 2 m (Notes H/Q).
- **Chassis replacement:** same manufacturer and same type only (R.112 proviso).
- **Fuel conversion (CNG/LPG/EV):** separately allowed under s.52(1) proviso 1 and R.112(1) with the applicable kit standards. Interaction with AIS-124 cl. 0.1 is unclear: AIS-124 bars fuel-system alteration *while building* the caravan. Status: PROFESSIONAL VERIFICATION REQUIRED.
- **Permit age limits (operational):** AITP is not granted after 12 years from first registration (AITP Rules 2023 r.4(5)), with tighter rules for diesel vehicles in the NCT of Delhi per the proviso. NCR end-of-life restrictions also apply (not researched in this pass).

## 8. Operation: registration, permits and licensing

- **Private use:** non-transport "Camper van or trailer for private use" (S.O. 1248(E)). No permit needed. The driver needs an LMV licence for M1/N1-derived vehicles up to 7,500 kg GVW, and a heavy passenger licence for M2/M3 per S.O. 2159(E). Validate heavier private M3 caravans; status CONDITIONAL.
- **Commercial use:**
  - Transport registration as "Camper vans or trailers"; fitness certificate; permit under MV Act s.66.
  - For interstate tourist operation, an **All India Tourist Permit** under **AITP (Permit) Rules 2023, G.S.R. 302(E), 18 Apr 2023**, in force **1 May 2023**. It superseded the AITP (Authorisation or Permit) Rules 2021.
  - Applies to a "tourist vehicle" under MV Act s.2(43).
  - Annual fees by passenger capacity excluding driver: under 5 seats ₹20,000; 5–9 seats ₹30,000; 10–22 seats ₹80,000; 23 or more ₹3,00,000. Free for battery and methanol/ethanol vehicles.
  - Validity: 90 days up to 5 years. Not granted after 12 years from first registration.
  - Source: https://transport.jharkhand.gov.in/pdf/All-India-Tourist-Vehicles-(Permit)-Rules-2023.pdf (Official statutory, Gazette copy mirrored by Jharkhand Transport Dept); MoRTH page https://morth.nic.in/hi/node/17129
  - **Caravans are not separately addressed** in the AITP Rules. Whether a camper van qualifies as a "tourist vehicle" (s.2(43): a contract carriage built to prescribed specifications) is UNKNOWN. Rule 128 tourist-vehicle specs exclude campers and house trailers.
- **Self-drive rental:** the Rent-a-Cab Scheme 1989 (as amended by S.O. 3202(E), 5 Aug 2021) applies only to motor cabs with tourist permits and operators of at least 50 cabs (per reprint). It is not a caravan route. Status: UNSUPPORTED/UNKNOWN.
