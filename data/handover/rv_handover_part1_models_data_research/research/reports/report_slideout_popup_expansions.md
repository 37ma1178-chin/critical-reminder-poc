# Parked-state expansions (slide-outs, pop-tops, awnings) under Indian rules (research report)

> Copied from the project chat as delivered (text chat, 20–21 Nov 2026 IST). Reproduced from the chat text visible to the packager; wording kept as delivered.

Indian law doesn't name slide-outs or pop-tops at all, so it neither bans nor allows them. The catch is that the size limits apply all the time, with no exception for a parked camper. That means an expanding camper is only safe if its expanded size also stays within the limits, or if the testing agency approves the design and records it on the registration.

**The size limits, CMVR Rule 93 (current text from GSR 414(E), 26 June 2020)**
- **Width and height:** at most 2.6 m wide and 4.0 m tall.
- **Length:** 12 m for vans and minibuses (M1/M2). Buses (M3) may be 13.5 m with two axles or 15 m with three.
- **Rear overhang:** the part behind the rear wheels can be at most 60% of the wheelbase.
- **Side projection:** nothing may stick out more than 355 mm past the rear wheel centre (single rear wheels) or 152 mm past the outer tyres (twin rear wheels). Mirrors and working indicators don't count.
- **Parked mode:** the rule says "shall not exceed" with no parked-mode exception. The only "travel mode" wording is for construction machines and harvesters, not campers.

**What's allowed**
- **Things not counted in the size:** side and rear foot steps, a folding "hood when down", and a roof ladder used while parked. That's Rule 93, Explanation II and 93(2). Whether a folded-down pop-top or a rolled-up awning counts as a "hood" is my reading, not confirmed anywhere.
- **Height exemption:** an RTO can exempt "special purpose vehicles" from the height limit by order. That covers height only, nothing is automatic, and I found no state that has done this for campers.
- **Stabiliser legs:** Maharashtra's caravan tourism policy (15 March 2021) actually asks for them on tourism-registered caravans. Kerala and Goa don't mention expansions at all.
- **Expanding designs in general:** a camper built with expanding parts can be legal if it gets type approval under AIS-124 and that approved design meets Rule 93.

**What's prohibited**
- **Going over the limits:** any expansion that takes the camper past the Rule 93 limits, since there's no parked-mode exception written anywhere.
- **Driving with anything sticking out:** you can't drive with anything sticking out past the body, or past the size on the registration certificate (RC). That's Rule 93(1)(B)(ii) and Driving Regulations 2017, regulation 35. A deployed awning, slide-out or raised pop-top while moving falls under this.
- **Unreported changes:** changing a vehicle's structure so it no longer matches its RC, unless it goes through the proper process. That's Motor Vehicles Act section 52. You must report the change and get the RC updated within 14 days.
- **DIY or unauthorised workshop work:** adding a slide-out or pop-top yourself, or at a workshop that isn't authorised. Changes to the body must be done by the manufacturer, its dealer or a state-authorised workshop, under Rule 112 (GSR 240(E), 2021).
- **Altering the base vehicle:** no changes to the chassis, suspension, brakes, fuel system or engine to build the camper. That's AIS-124 clause 0.1.

**Approval route for an expanding camper**
- **New build:** get type approval under Rule 125G and AIS-124, tested by ARAI, ICAT or another Rule 126 agency. AIS-124 Table 2 says a change in overall size has to be tested, and added parts that stick out need testing plus an assessment. The external-projection standard is IS 13942.
- **Design changes after approval:** tell the testing agency, which decides whether to extend the approval. That's AIS-124 clause 6.
- **Converting a registered vehicle:**
  - The base vehicle can be at most 3 years old.
  - The work must be done by an authorised builder.
  - The builder issues a Form 22F certificate, then you file Form 22D with the RTO within 14 days.

**Where the rules are silent**
- **No guidance on expansions:** there's no AIS-124 amendment on slide-outs, pop-tops, awnings, roof tents or stabiliser legs. MoRTH still lists the 2014 version with no amendments.
- **Which size to test:** nothing says whether the tested and registered size should be the folded size, the expanded size, or both. This is the biggest gap.
- **Roof tents and awnings:** nothing says whether they count as part of the body, as a load, or as a "hood".
- **Caravan parks:** nothing says whether expanding inside a caravan park, off the public road, is still bound by Rule 93.
- **No official approval found:** I found no ARAI or ICAT certificate for any pop-top or slide-out camper.

**What Indian makers claim (I couldn't verify any certificates)**
- **LuxeCamper:** press reports say it's ARAI-approved to AIS-124 and has a sensor awning. It isn't clear whether the awning was part of the approval.
- **Pinnacle Finetza:** built on the Force Traveller, it extends from 6,857 mm to 8,447 mm when parked, plus an awning. The article makes no approval claim.
- **EarthCamper:** claims ARAI approval for its rigid-body builds, with no slide-out or pop-top.
- **Motohom and JCBL:** I found no approved expanding models.
