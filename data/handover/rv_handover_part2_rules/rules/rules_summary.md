# Rules summary: Indian motor caravan / motorhome rules as measurable constraints

Prepared 21 Nov 2026 (IST) for the RV configurator. Plain-English summary, not legal advice.

- **Doc** = file in /rules/ (official_pdfs/, state_policies/, text_extracts/). "PDF p." = page of that PDF file, not the printed page number.
- **Checked** = clause text re-read from the document in this pass. **Earlier report** = taken from research/reports/ and not re-read here.
- Where the rules are silent, the row says **not specified**. Do not invent a limit for those in the configurator; show "no legal minimum".
- CMVR text was read from the Commercial Law Publishers annotated reprint (rules/text_extracts/cmvr_clp.txt, amended to GSR 337(E) of 28 Apr 2023) because the official MoRTH / India Code consolidated CMVR could not be downloaded (see MISSING in README). ## 9. M-category definitions and how they apply to the 14 variants

**Definitions** (AIS-053 cl. 3.1.1, PDF p.7; IS 14272:2011 cl. 3.1.1; CMVR Rule 2 refers to these AIS/IS definitions):
- **M** — motor vehicle with at least four wheels used for carrying passengers.
- **M1** — not more than **8 seats in addition to the driver's seat**. No GVW limit in these definitions.
- **M2** — **9 or more** seats in addition to the driver, **GVW ≤ 5 t**.
- **M3** — **9 or more** seats in addition to the driver, **GVW > 5 t**.
- **Motor caravan** — special purpose vehicle of category **M** (Rule 2(za), AIS-124 cl. 2.2, IS 14272 cl. 3.1.6.1). The older AIS-053 text (cl. 3.1.7.1, PDF p.9) says "special purpose **M1**"; the later documents say "category M", and AIS-124 itself gives separate requirements for M1 ≤2500 kg, M1 >2500 kg, M2 and M3 caravans.

**How it applies.** The as-sold category depends on seats and GVW. After conversion, the category follows the seats kept in the camper: a camper with **≤ 8 seats plus the driver is M1** whatever its GVW; with 9+ passenger seats it is M2 (GVW ≤ 5 t) or M3 (GVW > 5 t). The category matters for Rule 93 length (12 m vs 13.5 m), AIS-124 Table 2 test items, and bus-body rules (M3 emergency exit). RTO practice varies by state (open question).
